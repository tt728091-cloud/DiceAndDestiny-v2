Taxonomy VII

The sixteen combat types

Types are mechanical affinities that shape matchups and hook behavior. Creature-family traits such as Mummy, Dragon, Fae, Spider, Aquatic, and Airborne remain separate and can be combined with one primary and one optional secondary type.

Type before multiplier

Each type should first own a recognizable status, operation family, and hook pattern. The effectiveness chart comes after those identities are stable.

Type XVI · Fortune

The desired face

Curse makes a player fear rolling a marked face. Fortune creates the opposite tension: a temporary beneficial symbol is attached to a physical die face, making the player want to roll—and perhaps reroll—that die.

Shared baseline

Fortune generates Favor

Whenever a qualifying Fortune symbol activates, gain one Favor. Fortune cards, creatures, relics, and abilities can spend Favor to manipulate dice, prevent damage, protect cards, cleanse statuses, or improve ability tiers.

Cross-affinity design

How Fortune combines

Fortune becomes a genuine type when every other affinity can manipulate, exploit, accelerate, preserve, steal, or corrupt its Boons.

Creature concepts
  • Luck Sprite · Fortune

    Places one temporary Boon during Income; activation moves it to another die.

  • Gilded Scarab · Fortune/Swarm

    Spreads weak Boons across dice and triggers Jackpot after three different activations.

  • Fate Weaver · Fortune/Arcane

    Moves Boons and Curses between faces to create valuable but dangerous dice.

  • Fallen Seraph · Fortune/Shadow

    Creates powerful Tainted Boons whose activations also generate Debt.

  • Shrine Golem · Fortune/Stone

    Slowly builds durable Fortune symbols that convert Favor into armor.

  • Fortune Eater · Beast/Shadow

    Hunts the most blessed die and consumes any Boon left unused at round end.

Critical tension

Fortune + Curse

Place both overlays on the same valuable face. The result may complete an ultimate, grant Favor, and advance Curse simultaneously. The player is not choosing between a good die and a bad die; they are choosing whether the reward is worth engaging with the danger.

Fortune safeguards

01

Limited charges prevent infinite Favor and reroll loops.

02

Consequence-chain limits stop a Fortune-generated roll from retriggering the same Boon recursively.

03

Physical-roll provenance keeps copied faces separate from actual rolls.

04

Post-batch consequences resolve Favor and riders after the current dice batch finishes.

05

Shared overlay resolution applies Fortune identically to player rolls, status dice, defenses, and enemy D100 reconstruction.

Bestiary VIII

Creature synergy foundations

Each mechanic places persistent state on a shared game object or event stream so unrelated creature families can discover powerful interactions with it.

The design pattern

Attach state to something that travels. Dice, card instances, damage sources, statuses, links, tags, counters, and recorded events naturally cross multiple battle segments. A creature mechanic becomes highly mixable when other creatures can push those same objects through more hooks.

Strongest prototype foundations
  1. Haunted card instancesDeck · hand · damage reveal · discard · removal
  2. Web links between objectsDice · cards · statuses · combatants · sources
  3. Bargains and DebtImmediate power · Income collection · alternative payment
  4. Echoed eventsRecord · copy · replay · distinguish result from physical action
  5. Encounter-wide ResonanceShared meters that every creature family can feed or spend
Example mixed encounter

Specter · Spider · Fae

A Specter haunts a card. A Spider webs that card to a die. A Fae bargain improves the die but adds Debt whenever it is rolled. The haunt reacts to the webbed roll, prevention increases the Debt, and every trigger builds encounter Resonance.

Doctrine IX

Engine rules that preserve the magic

Combinatorial interactions stay understandable when the authority makes their timing and provenance explicit.

Current shared operations

  1. Record roll provenance.

    Offensive, voluntary reroll, forced reroll, defense, status, card, copied result.

  2. Separate event identities.

    A copied face is not a physical roll; a face change is not a reroll.

  3. Queue child consequences.

    Triggered damage begins after its parent roll batch, never in the middle.

  4. Limit repetitions.

    Support once per die, roll batch, resolution, checkpoint, or round.

  5. Persist overlays and history.

    Saves, snapshots, AI reconstruction, and replay must agree.

Highest-priority structural work

01

Universal checkpoint dispatcher for statuses, passives, cards, tokens, actors, and encounter rules.

02

Persistent die instances and overlays shared by player rolls, status rolls, defenses, and enemy reconstruction.

03

Converge settled and generic resolution so every proposal can reveal, react, revalidate, and commit consistently.

04

Stable typed timing using segment, phase, stage, and before/after position instead of scattered strings.
