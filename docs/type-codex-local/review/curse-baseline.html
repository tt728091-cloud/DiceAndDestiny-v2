Type XIV · Working template

Curse

Overwhelm physical dice with persistent negative face overlays, then use ordinary offense, defense, statuses, and forced rolls to make that corruption matter.

1⌁ 2 3⌁ 4 5⌁ 6
Identity doctrine

Corruption that travels with the die

Curse is persistent battle state attached to a physical die face. It does not disappear after activation and does not have default charges, weak or strong variants, or an automatic duration.

01

Persistent faces

A cursed face remains cursed for the battle until an explicit effect removes or moves it.

02

Every physical roll

Offense, rerolls, defense, Poison, cards, and application rolls all resolve face overlays.

03

Uncapped status stacks

Each trigger adds a Curse Count status stack to the afflicted combatant. The status has no stack limit and manifests during Ongoing Effects.

04

Seed face 1, then deepen

The first Curse always begins on face 1. Further corruption requires risky rolls and increasingly uncertain expansion.

05

Random by default

Ordinary applications do not let the Curse controller concentrate perfectly on one die.

06

Shared hooks

Curse becomes stronger when another type creates more rolls, subset selection, or die manipulation.

The core loop

Seed face 1 on clean dice → physically roll cursed dice → accumulate uncapped Curse Count status stacks on the combatant → manifest during Ongoing Effects → expand faces or force more rolls → pressure resources and future decisions.

What counts
  • Initial offensive rolls
  • Voluntary and forced rerolls
  • Defensive dice
  • Status rolls such as Poison
  • Card-created physical rolls
  • Lesser and Advanced Expand rolls
What does not count
  • Copied die results
  • Replayed final outcomes
  • Face changes without a roll
  • Echoes unless they explicitly roll a die
  • Qualification checks that merely inspect dice
Application resolver

Seed, Lesser Expand, Advanced Expand

Every Curse application follows the least concentrated legal path unless a stronger effect explicitly overrides targeting or expansion quality.

1
Clean dice remain

Seed Curse

Randomly select a clean die and place Curse directly on face 1. Baseline Seed does not roll the die. Face 1 is the default because lower-valued faces are generally less valuable, so the first infection begins as a lighter corruption.

2
Every die has Curse

Lesser Expand

Randomly select a cursed die with an uncursed face and roll it. Curse the result only if that face is currently clean. A cursed result adds one Curse Count status stack to the afflicted combatant but no new face.

3
Explicit advanced effect

Advanced Expand

Roll a cursed die, resolve its result, then choose one legal uncursed face adjacent to the result and curse it. A roll of 3 can target face 2 or face 4, never both. Advanced Expand adds at most one new cursed face.

4
Every face saturated

Curse Surge

Additional applications become forced physical rolls of random fully cursed dice, applying Curse Count status stacks instead of adding overlays.

Lesser Expand

Die has Curse on face 1

Roll 1: add one Curse Count status stack, no expansion.

Roll 4: curse face 4.

Maximum growth: one face.

Advanced Expand

Die has Curse on faces 1 and 3

Roll 3: add one Curse Count status stack; choose face 2 or face 4 and curse that one face.

Roll 4: face 3 is occupied, so face 5 is the only legal adjacent choice.

Maximum growth: one chosen adjacent face.

Seed-first priority
  1. For each granted application, recalculate legal dice.
  2. If a clean die exists, randomly select one and Seed Curse directly onto its face 1.
  3. Otherwise, randomly Lesser Expand an eligible cursed die.
  4. Advanced Expand is never automatic; content must explicitly grant it. It provides adjacent-face targeting, not two-face growth.
  5. Greater abilities may choose a die, override the default face-1 placement, choose between random candidates, or target the most/least cursed die.
Ongoing Effects checkpoint

Curse Count is an uncapped combatant status

Every cursed physical result immediately applies one Curse Count status stack to the afflicted combatant. The status is attached to that combatant—not to the die that generated it—and its consequences wait until a dedicated Curse Resolution window during Ongoing Effects.

Status rule · No limit

Most stackable statuses have a maximum stack limit. Curse Count is an explicit exception: it has no stack limit. Every valid application adds another status stack regardless of how many Curse Count stacks the afflicted combatant already has.

02Resolve Poison and other status rolls

Default manifestation

Every three remaining Curse Count status stacks produce one Curse damage.

Remove three stacks from the status per damage and preserve any remainder on the afflicted combatant. Three stacks produce one damage; four produce one damage with one stack remaining; six produce two damage.

Spend

Remove Curse Count status stacks before default damage to force rolls, Entomb dice, expand Curse, or empower the Curse creature.

Replace

Convert groups into another consequence—reduced rolls, weakened defense, delayed Income loss, or expansion—instead of damage.

Observe

Check the status stack total without removing it. Observer effects should be smaller because those same stacks can still become damage.

Force the target to physically roll selected cursed dice. Every Curse result applies one Curse Count status stack to that target; the final stack total may weaken defense, apply Blind, or feed the next Ongoing manifestation.

Choose one face number and place Curse on that face of every clean target die. It does not force an immediate roll; it removes clean-die safety from future random offense, defense, card, and status selection.

Defensive · ICorruptive defense

Hexward Rebuttal

When attacked, apply one Seed-first Curse application to the attacker. A randomly selected clean die receives Curse on face 1 without a roll; if every die is already seeded, roll a random eligible die and Lesser Expand instead. Any existing Curse rolled during expansion applies a Curse Count status stack normally.

Defensive · IIRetaliation

Misfortune Repaid

Reduce part of a finalized incoming source, then apply Curse pressure to a die that helped qualify it. Higher tiers may choose the die, Entomb it, or protect the new Curse from immediate cleansing.

Damage-focused

Normal damage with zero or one resistible Curse application.

Hybrid

Moderate damage with two or three randomly targeted applications.

Corruption-focused

Little damage with many applications, Advanced Expand, or targeting control.

Manifestation-focused

Little damage and little new Curse; cash in setup through Curse Count status stacks, forced rolls, and Entombment.

Twenty-four standalone Curse cards

Every card in the foundational pool must function with Curse alone. Pair-specific cards belong to a separate Fusion pool so they never become dead draws in single-type decks.

Future resistance design

Counterplay is intentionally external for now

Curse currently has no universal built-in resistance, Resolve resource, or automatic cleansing action. Other types, cards, abilities, relics, or later system work may provide those tools.

Not a base rule

The following Purge Roll is a preserved design idea for counter-Curse content. Characters cannot perform it automatically unless an effect grants it.

Candidate Purge Roll

The corruption helps reveal itself

  1. An ability or card grants a Purge Roll.
  2. Choose one cursed physical die.
  3. Physically roll that die and resolve its overlays.
  4. If the rolled face is cursed, remove Curse from that face.
  5. If the rolled face is clean, the purge does nothing.

A lightly cursed die takes longer to purify because finding its single corrupted face is difficult. A heavily cursed die fights back more reliably because almost any result identifies removable corruption.

Why no universal resistance yet?

Leaving resistance outside the base Curse rules lets the Curse type push meaningful volume. Counterplay then becomes deckbuilding, type selection, card timing, and resource management rather than a free automatic reduction attached to every target.
