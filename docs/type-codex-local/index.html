The core loop

Apply Burn or another damaging status → build Heat to three → keep the attack bonus, or spend Heat before it cools → mark discarded cards with Ember to rebuild.

Stoke → Cinderlash → maintain Heat

Stoke provides 2 Heat. Cinderlash applies Burn, which can replenish Heat after it cools. Ember offers a second way to reach the three-Heat attack bonus.

Dry Tinder → Poison or Burn → Bellows

Kindling strengthens the next status damage. Bellows spends Heat to prepare more Kindling and possibly Burn, trading your immediate Heat threshold for later pressure.

How these rules work

Type-specific limits & balance

Heat gives a single +1 attack bonus at three or four stacks; it does not add damage per stack. Burn at three stacks threatens 3 total damage before expiring. Kindling adds at most 1 during an Ongoing Effects segment. Values still need playtesting.

Worked interaction

You enter Ongoing Effects with 3 Heat and lose 1, leaving 2. The enemy takes Poison damage, earning your once-per-round Heat and returning you to 3. Your next attack gains 1 damage. Spending Heat before the attack may lose that bonus.

Battle Hooks Field Guide · Turn structure

Shared play rules: timing, dice, costs and recovery

These rules explain the proposed mixed types. Curse and Venom keep their reviewed rules. The new statuses and mixed-dice behavior still need approval and implementation.

Reading these rules

You means the player using the type or the effect that placed a mark. This enemy means the creature carrying the harmful status. Gain puts stacks on you; apply puts them on the chosen enemy. A cap is the most stacks allowed. Keep unused stacks unless the rule says when to remove them.

When cards can be used

Action: during your Offensive planning, before choosing your ability. Offensive reaction: after the combat dice and chosen abilities are revealed, before attacks. Defensive reaction: after a Defensive ability is rolled. Roll reaction: after dice are rolled for a status, defense or card, before using their results. Application reaction: before a new status or mark is added. Damage reaction: after incoming damage and the health cards at risk are shown, before those cards are removed.

Playing and paying

Playing a card means using it from your hand, paying its Energy and any other listed costs, and putting it in discard. This counts as a play even if the opponent later cancels its effect. A card you cannot afford is not played and costs nothing. Echo repeating damage, a status activating, drawing a card and discarding a card are not plays from your hand. Pay optional ability costs when choosing the ability; resources it will give you afterward cannot pay those costs.

One creature when types mix

A mixed creature still uses five combat dice and chooses one Offensive ability. Its chosen types each provide their listed ways to earn resources, even when it has zero stacks. Gaining a status from another type gives that status its printed uses, but does not grant that other type’s separate rewards: gaining Armor does not also give the Stone way to earn Resolve. Card pools are options for building a creature, not a starting deck containing every card.

Numbers and symbols

These types use faces 1–3 for their first symbol, 4–5 for their second, and 6 for their third. Symbols belong to their type: Blood Fang is not Venom Fang. A matching-number or straight recipe can use dice from any type. Three equal numbers means at least three matching dice. A four-number straight is 1–4, 2–5 or 3–6. A die supplies only one required symbol in a recipe, even if Graft gives it another option. Rune stores a number; it does not add a symbol.

Rolling versus changing a number

Rolling a die can activate a marked face such as Curse or Boon, even if that number is later rerolled. Changing the number without rolling does not activate either mark. Use the final number for the normal status, defense or card result, including Conductive. A die rolled for one effect may be rerolled at most once and have its number changed at most once. Catalyst acts automatically before optional Soaked retries. Neither can reroll a die the other already retried.

Which die carries a mark

Combat rolls use your five combat dice. A card such as Pulse that tells an enemy to roll a combat die uses that die and its marks; the extra roll does not change their saved combat results. A Defensive ability instead uses fresh dice of its named type. Proposed mixed Curse/Venom creatures will also roll their marked combat dice for toxins: assign checks to dice 1 through 5 in order, then start again at die 1 if more checks are needed. That mixed-dice rule is a design proposal; playable Venom currently uses separate effect dice.

Extra rolls and locked dice

Normal combat rolls and their rerolls use the game’s roll limit. Overload adds one attempt, up to four total including the first roll. Entangle uses its existing reduction. Chill instead charges 1 Energy to begin rerolling. Scent explicitly allows one extra single-die attempt after the normal attempts. It can earn Charge and affect Streak, but cannot reroll a Frostbound die.

Attacks, follow-ups and prevention

An Offensive ability is your attack. Damaging Action cards join the attacks due to resolve and remain separate hits; they do not give extra ability selections. Use your one Defensive ability against one attack or damaging card. Echo, Pulse, Brood and Pounce happen as their text directs, allow damage prevention, and do not grant another Defensive roll. For one incoming hit, apply rolled defense and protective statuses first, then any halving effect, then damage-reaction card prevention.

Applying a status with an attack

Blocking attack damage does not also stop statuses or resources the ability grants. A reaction that cancels the whole ability stops those effects too. Unless the text specifically puts a mark before damage, a newly applied Blood Mark, Web or similar mark watches later damage, not the attack that just placed it. Effects saying after the attack happen after its damage and attached statuses finish.

Rewards and limits

Earn prevention rewards only for damage you actually stop: preventing 3 against a 1-damage hit counts as 1. Damage rewards require damage that gets through. Each type can earn its own reward from the same event, but its per-round limit still applies. A new reward becomes available after the action that earned it. It cannot pay for that same action. An extra damage effect cannot repeatedly activate itself through other extra damage effects.

Stack limits, expiry and cleansing

Extra stacks above the cap are lost unless a rule says otherwise; Poison retains its reviewed Incubation overflow rule. Adding a stack does not refresh older stacks. Next Offensive or next Ongoing Effects means the next one that starts after application. Following round means the round after the current one. Remove unused timed stacks when their stated expiry arrives. Cleanse removes an existing stack. Stopping a new stack prevents its application; it is not a cleanse. Once a status has produced a visible roll or damage to answer, removing the status does not undo that pending effect.

Card and die marks

Enemy card marks may target the discard pile or cards already revealed by damage, never a hidden hand or deck. Drawing a marked card does not reveal the rest of the hand. Moving a mark keeps its remaining uses and expiry. A face or card may have both a helpful and harmful mark. Removing one named mark leaves the others. A normal status cleanse does not erase card marks, die marks, Corpse records, Echo Records or installed Modules unless the effect explicitly names that kind of target.

Recovery and sacrifice

One health card is one card in your hand, deck or discard pile. Recovery returns a card previously lost to damage to your discard pile; it is not a draw. Cards sacrificed, paid as costs or otherwise removed without damage cannot be recovered. Recover at most two cards per round across all types. Choose cards already lost before the current attack or damage began, and reserve the recovery when you pay: a failed paid attempt still uses that part of the two-card limit. Do not pay for recovery when there is no card to choose. Recovering a card erases its Corpse token.

Last chances

Sacrifice permanently removes an additional card from your hand or discard pile as a cost. It cannot be the card being played or a card the incoming damage would already remove, and it must leave you at least one health card. Death Anchor can save you once per battle by returning an earlier damage loss before defeat is decided; it also needs a remaining recovery use that round. Beacon instead selects your next Income draw. Cards cannot be played after defeat.

Transfers and redirects

Moving a status removes it from one creature and puts it on the other. If the destination cannot receive it, leave it where it was. Moving never duplicates a stack or refreshes its expiry. Redirecting damage moves the stated amount to the enemy instead of you; it keeps its damage type, allows prevention, and does not grant another defense roll. The same point of damage cannot be redirected twice.

Defensive reaction: after a Defensive ability is rolled. Roll reaction: after dice are rolled for a status, defense or card, before using their results. Application reaction: before a new status or mark is added. Damage reaction: after incoming damage and the health cards at risk are shown, before those cards are removed.

Playing and paying

Playing a card means using it from your hand, paying its Energy and any other listed costs, and putting it in discard. This counts as a play even if the opponent later cancels its effect. A card you cannot afford is not played and costs nothing. Echo repeating damage, a status activating, drawing a card and discarding a card are not plays from your hand. Pay optional ability costs when choosing the ability; resources it will give you afterward cannot pay those costs.

One creature when types mix

A mixed creature still uses five combat dice and chooses one Offensive ability. Its chosen types each provide their listed ways to earn resources, even when it has zero stacks. Gaining a status from another type gives that status its printed uses, but does not grant that other type’s separate rewards: gaining Armor does not also give the Stone way to earn Resolve. Card pools are options for building a creature, not a starting deck containing every card.

Numbers and symbols

These types use faces 1–3 for their first symbol, 4–5 for their second, and 6 for their third. Symbols belong to their type: Blood Fang is not Venom Fang. A matching-number or straight recipe can use dice from any type. Three equal numbers means at least three matching dice. A four-number straight is 1–4, 2–5 or 3–6. A die supplies only one required symbol in a recipe, even if Graft gives it another option. Rune stores a number; it does not add a symbol.

Rolling versus changing a number

Rolling a die can activate a marked face such as Curse or Boon, even if that number is later rerolled. Changing the number without rolling does not activate either mark. Use the final number for the normal status, defense or card result, including Conductive. A die rolled for one effect may be rerolled at most once and have its number changed at most once. Catalyst acts automatically before optional Soaked retries. Neither can reroll a die the other already retried.

Which die carries a mark

Combat rolls use your five combat dice. A card such as Pulse that tells an enemy to roll a combat die uses that die and its marks; the extra roll does not change their saved combat results. A Defensive ability instead uses fresh dice of its named type. Proposed mixed Curse/Venom creatures will also roll their marked combat dice for toxins: assign checks to dice 1 through 5 in order, then start again at die 1 if more checks are needed. That mixed-dice rule is a design proposal; playable Venom currently uses separate effect dice.

Extra rolls and locked dice

Normal combat rolls and their rerolls use the game’s roll limit. Overload adds one attempt, up to four total including the first roll. Entangle uses its existing reduction. Chill instead charges 1 Energy to begin rerolling. Scent explicitly allows one extra single-die attempt after the normal attempts. It can earn Charge and affect Streak, but cannot reroll a Frostbound die.

Attacks, follow-ups and prevention

An Offensive ability is your attack. Damaging Action cards join the attacks due to resolve and remain separate hits; they do not give extra ability selections. Use your one Defensive ability against one attack or damaging card. Echo, Pulse, Brood and Pounce happen as their text directs, allow damage prevention, and do not grant another Defensive roll. For one incoming hit, apply rolled defense and protective statuses first, then any halving effect, then damage-reaction card prevention.

Applying a status with an attack

Blocking attack damage does not also stop statuses or resources the ability grants. A reaction that cancels the whole ability stops those effects too. Unless the text specifically puts a mark before damage, a newly applied Blood Mark, Web or similar mark watches later damage, not the attack that just placed it. Effects saying after the attack happen after its damage and attached statuses finish.

Rewards and limits

Earn prevention rewards only for damage you actually stop: preventing 3 against a 1-damage hit counts as 1. Damage rewards require damage that gets through. Each type can earn its own reward from the same event, but its per-round limit still applies. A new reward becomes available after the action that earned it. It cannot pay for that same action. An extra damage effect cannot repeatedly activate itself through other extra damage effects.

Stack limits, expiry and cleansing

Extra stacks above the cap are lost unless a rule says otherwise; Poison retains its reviewed Incubation overflow rule. Adding a stack does not refresh older stacks. Next Offensive or next Ongoing Effects means the next one that starts after application. Following round means the round after the current one. Remove unused timed stacks when their stated expiry arrives. Cleanse removes an existing stack. Stopping a new stack prevents its application; it is not a cleanse. Once a status has produced a visible roll or damage to answer, removing the status does not undo that pending effect.

Card and die marks

Enemy card marks may target the discard pile or cards already revealed by damage, never a hidden hand or deck. Drawing a marked card does not reveal the rest of the hand. Moving a mark keeps its remaining uses and expiry. A face or card may have both a helpful and harmful mark. Removing one named mark leaves the others. A normal status cleanse does not erase card marks, die marks, Corpse records, Echo Records or installed Modules unless the effect explicitly names that kind of target.

Recovery and sacrifice

One health card is one card in your hand, deck or discard pile. Recovery returns a card previously lost to damage to your discard pile; it is not a draw. Cards sacrificed, paid as costs or otherwise removed without damage cannot be recovered. Recover at most two cards per round across all types. Choose cards already lost before the current attack or damage began, and reserve the recovery when you pay: a failed paid attempt still uses that part of the two-card limit. Do not pay for recovery when there is no card to choose. Recovering a card erases its Corpse token.

Last chances

Sacrifice permanently removes an additional card from your hand or discard pile as a cost. It cannot be the card being played or a card the incoming damage would already remove, and it must leave you at least one health card. Death Anchor can save you once per battle by returning an earlier damage loss before defeat is decided; it also needs a remaining recovery use that round. Beacon instead selects your next Income draw. Cards cannot be played after defeat.

Transfers and redirects

Moving a status removes it from one creature and puts it on the other. If the destination cannot receive it, leave it where it was. Moving never duplicates a stack or refreshes its expiry. Redirecting damage moves the stated amount to the enemy instead of you; it keeps its damage type, allows prevention, and does not grant another defense roll. The same point of damage cannot be redirected twice.

Battle Hooks Field Guide · Turn structure

Shared play rules: timing, dice, costs and recovery

These rules explain the proposed mixed types. Curse and Venom keep their reviewed rules. The new statuses and mixed-dice behavior still need approval and implementation.

Reading these rules

You means the player using the type or the effect that placed a mark. This enemy means the creature carrying the harmful status. Gain puts stacks on you; apply puts them on the chosen enemy. A cap is the most stacks allowed. Keep unused stacks unless the rule says when to remove them.

When cards can be used

Action: during your Offensive planning, before choosing your ability. Offensive reaction: after the combat dice and chosen abilities are revealed, before attacks. Defensive reaction: after a Defensive ability is rolled. Roll reaction: after dice are rolled for a status, defense or card, before using their results. Application reaction: before a new status or mark is added. Damage reaction: after incoming damage and the health cards at risk are shown, before those cards are removed.

Playing and paying

Playing a card means using it from your hand, paying its Energy and any other listed costs, and putting it in discard. This counts as a play even if the opponent later cancels its effect. A card you cannot afford is not played and costs nothing. Echo repeating damage, a status activating, drawing a card and discarding a card are not plays from your hand. Pay optional ability costs when choosing the ability; resources it will give you afterward cannot pay those costs.

One creature when types mix

A mixed creature still uses five combat dice and chooses one Offensive ability. Its chosen types each provide their listed ways to earn resources, even when it has zero stacks. Gaining a status from another type gives that status its printed uses, but does not grant that other type’s separate rewards: gaining Armor does not also give the Stone way to earn Resolve. Card pools are options for building a creature, not a starting deck containing every card.

Numbers and symbols

These types use faces 1–3 for their first symbol, 4–5 for their second, and 6 for their third. Symbols belong to their type: Blood Fang is not Venom Fang. A matching-number or straight recipe can use dice from any type. Three equal numbers means at least three matching dice. A four-number straight is 1–4, 2–5 or 3–6. A die supplies only one required symbol in a recipe, even if Graft gives it another option. Rune stores a number; it does not add a symbol.

Rolling versus changing a number

Rolling a die can activate a marked face such as Curse or Boon, even if that number is later rerolled. Changing the number without rolling does not activate either mark. Use the final number for the normal status, defense or card result, including Conductive. A die rolled for one effect may be rerolled at most once and have its number changed at most once. Catalyst acts automatically before optional Soaked retries. Neither can reroll a die the other already retried.

Which die carries a mark

Combat rolls use your five combat dice. A card such as Pulse that tells an enemy to roll a combat die uses that die and its marks; the extra roll does not change their saved combat results. A Defensive ability instead uses fresh dice of its named type. Proposed mixed Curse/Venom creatures will also roll their marked combat dice for toxins: assign checks to dice 1 through 5 in order, then start again at die 1 if more checks are needed. That mixed-dice rule is a design proposal; playable Venom currently uses separate effect dice.

Extra rolls and locked dice

Normal combat rolls and their rerolls use the game’s roll limit. Overload adds one attempt, up to four total including the first roll. Entangle uses its existing reduction. Chill instead charges 1 Energy to begin rerolling. Scent explicitly allows one extra single-die attempt after the normal attempts. It can earn Charge and affect Streak, but cannot reroll a Frostbound die.

Attacks, follow-ups and prevention

An Offensive ability is your attack. Damaging Action cards join the attacks due to resolve and remain separate hits; they do not give extra ability selections. Use your one Defensive ability against one attack or damaging card. Echo, Pulse, Brood and Pounce happen as their text directs, allow damage prevention, and do not grant another Defensive roll. For one incoming hit, apply rolled defense and protective statuses first, then any halving effect, then damage-reaction card prevention.

Applying a status with an attack

Blocking attack damage does not also stop statuses or resources the ability grants. A reaction that cancels the whole ability stops those effects too. Unless the text specifically puts a mark before damage, a newly applied Blood Mark, Web or similar mark watches later damage, not the attack that just placed it. Effects saying after the attack happen after its damage and attached statuses finish.

Rewards and limits

Earn prevention rewards only for damage you actually stop: preventing 3 against a 1-damage hit counts as 1. Damage rewards require damage that gets through. Each type can earn its own reward from the same event, but its per-round limit still applies. A new reward becomes available after the action that earned it. It cannot pay for that same action. An extra damage effect cannot repeatedly activate itself through other extra damage effects.

Stack limits, expiry and cleansing

Extra stacks above the cap are lost unless a rule says otherwise; Poison retains its reviewed Incubation overflow rule. Adding a stack does not refresh older stacks. Next Offensive or next Ongoing Effects means the next one that starts after application. Following round means the round after the current one. Remove unused timed stacks when their stated expiry arrives. Cleanse removes an existing stack. Stopping a new stack prevents its application; it is not a cleanse. Once a status has produced a visible roll or damage to answer, removing the status does not undo that pending effect.

Card and die marks

Enemy card marks may target the discard pile or cards already revealed by damage, never a hidden hand or deck. Drawing a marked card does not reveal the rest of the hand. Moving a mark keeps its remaining uses and expiry. A face or card may have both a helpful and harmful mark. Removing one named mark leaves the others. A normal status cleanse does not erase card marks, die marks, Corpse records, Echo Records or installed Modules unless the effect explicitly names that kind of target.

Recovery and sacrifice

One health card is one card in your hand, deck or discard pile. Recovery returns a card previously lost to damage to your discard pile; it is not a draw. Cards sacrificed, paid as costs or otherwise removed without damage cannot be recovered. Recover at most two cards per round across all types. Choose cards already lost before the current attack or damage began, and reserve the recovery when you pay: a failed paid attempt still uses that part of the two-card limit. Do not pay for recovery when there is no card to choose. Recovering a card erases its Corpse token.

Last chances

Sacrifice permanently removes an additional card from your hand or discard pile as a cost. It cannot be the card being played or a card the incoming damage would already remove, and it must leave you at least one health card. Death Anchor can save you once per battle by returning an earlier damage loss before defeat is decided; it also needs a remaining recovery use that round. Beacon instead selects your next Income draw. Cards cannot be played after defeat.

Transfers and redirects

Moving a status removes it from one creature and puts it on the other. If the destination cannot receive it, leave it where it was. Moving never duplicates a stack or refreshes its expiry. Redirecting damage moves the stated amount to the enemy instead of you; it keeps its damage type, allows prevention, and does not grant another defense roll. The same point of damage cannot be redirected twice.

Battle Hooks Field Guide · Turn structure

Shared play rules: timing, dice, costs and recovery

These rules explain the proposed mixed types. Curse and Venom keep their reviewed rules. The new statuses and mixed-dice behavior still need approval and implementation.

Reading these rules

You means the player using the type or the effect that placed a mark. This enemy means the creature carrying the harmful status. Gain puts stacks on you; apply puts them on the chosen enemy. A cap is the most stacks allowed. Keep unused stacks unless the rule says when to remove them.

When cards can be used

Action: during your Offensive planning, before choosing your ability. Offensive reaction: after the combat dice and chosen abilities are revealed, before attacks. Defensive reaction: after a Defensive ability is rolled. Roll reaction: after dice are rolled for a status, defense or card, before using their results. Application reaction: before a new status or mark is added. Damage reaction: after incoming damage and the health cards at risk are shown, before those cards are removed.

Playing and paying

Playing a card means using it from your hand, paying its Energy and any other listed costs, and putting it in discard. This counts as a play even if the opponent later cancels its effect. A card you cannot afford is not played and costs nothing. Echo repeating damage, a status activating, drawing a card and discarding a card are not plays from your hand. Pay optional ability costs when choosing the ability; resources it will give you afterward cannot pay those costs.

One creature when types mix

A mixed creature still uses five combat dice and chooses one Offensive ability. Its chosen types each provide their listed ways to earn resources, even when it has zero stacks. Gaining a status from another type gives that status its printed uses, but does not grant that other type’s separate rewards: gaining Armor does not also give the Stone way to earn Resolve. Card pools are options for building a creature, not a starting deck containing every card.

Numbers and symbols

These types use faces 1–3 for their first symbol, 4–5 for their second, and 6 for their third. Symbols belong to their type: Blood Fang is not Venom Fang. A matching-number or straight recipe can use dice from any type. Three equal numbers means at least three matching dice. A four-number straight is 1–4, 2–5 or 3–6. A die supplies only one required symbol in a recipe, even if Graft gives it another option. Rune stores a number; it does not add a symbol.

Rolling versus changing a number

Rolling a die can activate a marked face such as Curse or Boon, even if that number is later rerolled. Changing the number without rolling does not activate either mark. Use the final number for the normal status, defense or card result, including Conductive. A die rolled for one effect may be rerolled at most once and have its number changed at most once. Catalyst acts automatically before optional Soaked retries. Neither can reroll a die the other already retried.

Which die carries a mark

Combat rolls use your five combat dice. A card such as Pulse that tells an enemy to roll a combat die uses that die and its marks; the extra roll does not change their saved combat results. A Defensive ability instead uses fresh dice of its named type. Proposed mixed Curse/Venom creatures will also roll their marked combat dice for toxins: assign checks to dice 1 through 5 in order, then start again at die 1 if more checks are needed. That mixed-dice rule is a design proposal; playable Venom currently uses separate effect dice.

Extra rolls and locked dice

Normal combat rolls and their rerolls use the game’s roll limit. Overload adds one attempt, up to four total including the first roll. Entangle uses its existing reduction. Chill instead charges 1 Energy to begin rerolling. Scent explicitly allows one extra single-die attempt after the normal attempts. It can earn Charge and affect Streak, but cannot reroll a Frostbound die.

Attacks, follow-ups and prevention

An Offensive ability is your attack. Damaging Action cards join the attacks due to resolve and remain separate hits; they do not give extra ability selections. Use your one Defensive ability against one attack or damaging card. Echo, Pulse, Brood and Pounce happen as their text directs, allow damage prevention, and do not grant another Defensive roll. For one incoming hit, apply rolled defense and protective statuses first, then any halving effect, then damage-reaction card prevention.

Applying a status with an attack

Blocking attack damage does not also stop statuses or resources the ability grants. A reaction that cancels the whole ability stops those effects too. Unless the text specifically puts a mark before damage, a newly applied Blood Mark, Web or similar mark watches later damage, not the attack that just placed it. Effects saying after the attack happen after its damage and attached statuses finish.

Rewards and limits

Earn prevention rewards only for damage you actually stop: preventing 3 against a 1-damage hit counts as 1. Damage rewards require damage that gets through. Each type can earn its own reward from the same event, but its per-round limit still applies. A new reward becomes available after the action that earned it. It cannot pay for that same action. An extra damage effect cannot repeatedly activate itself through other extra damage effects.

Stack limits, expiry and cleansing

Extra stacks above the cap are lost unless a rule says otherwise; Poison retains its reviewed Incubation overflow rule. Adding a stack does not refresh older stacks. Next Offensive or next Ongoing Effects means the next one that starts after application. Following round means the round after the current one. Remove unused timed stacks when their stated expiry arrives. Cleanse removes an existing stack. Stopping a new stack prevents its application; it is not a cleanse. Once a status has produced a visible roll or damage to answer, removing the status does not undo that pending effect.

Card and die marks

Enemy card marks may target the discard pile or cards already revealed by damage, never a hidden hand or deck. Drawing a marked card does not reveal the rest of the hand. Moving a mark keeps its remaining uses and expiry. A face or card may have both a helpful and harmful mark. Removing one named mark leaves the others. A normal status cleanse does not erase card marks, die marks, Corpse records, Echo Records or installed Modules unless the effect explicitly names that kind of target.

Recovery and sacrifice

One health card is one card in your hand, deck or discard pile. Recovery returns a card previously lost to damage to your discard pile; it is not a draw. Cards sacrificed, paid as costs or otherwise removed without damage cannot be recovered. Recover at most two cards per round across all types. Choose cards already lost before the current attack or damage began, and reserve the recovery when you pay: a failed paid attempt still uses that part of the two-card limit. Do not pay for recovery when there is no card to choose. Recovering a card erases its Corpse token.

Last chances

Sacrifice permanently removes an additional card from your hand or discard pile as a cost. It cannot be the card being played or a card the incoming damage would already remove, and it must leave you at least one health card. Death Anchor can save you once per battle by returning an earlier damage loss before defeat is decided; it also needs a remaining recovery use that round. Beacon instead selects your next Income draw. Cards cannot be played after defeat.

Transfers and redirects

Moving a status removes it from one creature and puts it on the other. If the destination cannot receive it, leave it where it was. Moving never duplicates a stack or refreshes its expiry. Redirecting damage moves the stated amount to the enemy instead of you; it keeps its damage type, allows prevention, and does not grant another defense roll. The same point of damage cannot be redirected twice.

Blocking attack damage does not also stop statuses or resources the ability grants. A reaction that cancels the whole ability stops those effects too. Rewards and limits

Earn prevention rewards only for damage you actually stop: preventing 3 against a 1-damage hit counts as 1. Damage rewards require damage that gets through. Stack limits, expiry and cleansing

Extra stacks above the cap are lost unless a rule says otherwise; Poison retains its reviewed Incubation overflow rule. Adding a stack does not refresh older stacks. Card and die marks

Enemy card marks may target the discard pile or cards already revealed by damage, never a hidden hand or deck. Drawing a marked card does not reveal the rest of the hand. Recovery and sacrifice

One health card is one card in your hand, deck or discard pile. Recovery returns a card previously lost to damage to your discard pile; it is not a draw. Cards sacrificed, paid as costs or otherwise removed without damage cannot be recovered. Recover at most two cards per round across all types. Choose cards already lost before the current attack or damage began, and reserve the recovery when you pay: a failed paid attempt still uses that part of the two-card limit. Do not pay for recovery when there is no card to choose. Recovering a card erases its Corpse token.

Sacrifice permanently removes an additional card from your hand or discard pile as a cost. It cannot be the card being played or a card the incoming damage would already remove, and it must leave you at least one health card. Death Anchor can save you once per battle by returning an earlier damage loss before defeat is decided; it also needs a remaining recovery use that round. Transfers and redirects

Moving a status removes it from one creature and puts it on the other. If the destination cannot receive it, leave it where it was. Moving never duplicates a stack or refreshes its expiry. Redirecting damage moves the stated amount to the enemy instead of you; it keeps its damage type, allows prevention, and does not grant another defense roll. Reading these rules

Playing and paying

Playing a card means using it from your hand, paying its Energy and any other listed costs, and putting it in discard. This counts as a play even if the opponent later cancels its effect. The first time each round you discard a card during Offensive, gain 1 Brood. Playing a card does not count as discarding it for this rule. Before your Offensive attack, you may spend up to 2 Brood. Web

Connect separate small hits, even across different types.

Shared play rules: timing, dice, costs and recovery

These rules explain the proposed mixed types. Curse and Venom keep their reviewed rules. The new statuses and mixed-dice behavior still need approval and implementation.

Reading these rules

You means the player using the type or the effect that placed a mark. This enemy means the creature carrying the harmful status. Gain puts stacks on you; apply puts them on the chosen enemy. A cap is the most stacks allowed. Keep unused stacks unless the rule says when to remove them.

When cards can be used

Action: during your Offensive planning, before choosing your ability. Offensive reaction: after the combat dice and chosen abilities are revealed, before attacks. Defensive reaction: after a Defensive ability is rolled. Roll reaction: after dice are rolled for a status, defense or card, before using their results. Application reaction: before a new status or mark is added. Damage reaction: after incoming damage and the health cards at risk are shown, before those cards are removed.

Playing and paying

Playing a card means using it from your hand, paying its Energy and any other listed costs, and putting it in discard. This counts as a play even if the opponent later cancels its effect. A card you cannot afford is not played and costs nothing. Echo repeating damage, a status activating, drawing a card and discarding a card are not plays from your hand. Pay optional ability costs when choosing the ability; resources it will give you afterward cannot pay those costs.

One creature when types mix

A mixed creature still uses five combat dice and chooses one Offensive ability. Its chosen types each provide their listed ways to earn resources, even when it has zero stacks. Gaining a status from another type gives that status its printed uses, but does not grant that other type’s separate rewards: gaining Armor does not also give the Stone way to earn Resolve. Card pools are options for building a creature, not a starting deck containing every card.

Numbers and symbols

These types use faces 1–3 for their first symbol, 4–5 for their second, and 6 for their third. Symbols belong to their type: Blood Fang is not Venom Fang. A matching-number or straight recipe can use dice from any type. Three equal numbers means at least three matching dice. A four-number straight is 1–4, 2–5 or 3–6. A die supplies only one required symbol in a recipe, even if Graft gives it another option. Rune stores a number; it does not add a symbol.

Rolling versus changing a number

Rolling a die can activate a marked face such as Curse or Boon, even if that number is later rerolled. Changing the number without rolling does not activate either mark. Use the final number for the normal status, defense or card result, including Conductive. A die rolled for one effect may be rerolled at most once and have its number changed at most once. Catalyst acts automatically before optional Soaked retries. Neither can reroll a die the other already retried.

Which die carries a mark

Combat rolls use your five combat dice. A card such as Pulse that tells an enemy to roll a combat die uses that die and its marks; the extra roll does not change their saved combat results. A Defensive ability instead uses fresh dice of its named type. Proposed mixed Curse/Venom creatures will also roll their marked combat dice for toxins: assign checks to dice 1 through 5 in order, then start again at die 1 if more checks are needed. That mixed-dice rule is a design proposal; playable Venom currently uses separate effect dice.

Extra rolls and locked dice

Normal combat rolls and their rerolls use the game’s roll limit. Overload adds one attempt, up to four total including the first roll. Entangle uses its existing reduction. Chill instead charges 1 Energy to begin rerolling. Scent explicitly allows one extra single-die attempt after the normal attempts. It can earn Charge and affect Streak, but cannot reroll a Frostbound die.

Attacks, follow-ups and prevention

An Offensive ability is your attack. Damaging Action cards join the attacks due to resolve and remain separate hits; they do not give extra ability selections. Use your one Defensive ability against one attack or damaging card. Echo, Pulse, Brood and Pounce happen as their text directs, allow damage prevention, and do not grant another Defensive roll. For one incoming hit, apply rolled defense and protective statuses first, then any halving effect, then damage-reaction card prevention.

Applying a status with an attack

Blocking attack damage does not also stop statuses or resources the ability grants. A reaction that cancels the whole ability stops those effects too. Unless the text specifically puts a mark before damage, a newly applied Blood Mark, Web or similar mark watches later damage, not the attack that just placed it. Effects saying after the attack happen after its damage and attached statuses finish.

Rewards and limits

Earn prevention rewards only for damage you actually stop: preventing 3 against a 1-damage hit counts as 1. Damage rewards require damage that gets through. Each type can earn its own reward from the same event, but its per-round limit still applies. A new reward becomes available after the action that earned it. It cannot pay for that same action. An extra damage effect cannot repeatedly activate itself through other extra damage effects.

Stack limits, expiry and cleansing

Extra stacks above the cap are lost unless a rule says otherwise; Poison retains its reviewed Incubation overflow rule. Adding a stack does not refresh older stacks. Next Offensive or next Ongoing Effects means the next one that starts after application. Following round means the round after the current one. Remove unused timed stacks when their stated expiry arrives. Cleanse removes an existing stack. Stopping a new stack prevents its application; it is not a cleanse. Once a status has produced a visible roll or damage to answer, removing the status does not undo that pending effect.

Card and die marks

Enemy card marks may target the discard pile or cards already revealed by damage, never a hidden hand or deck. Drawing a marked card does not reveal the rest of the hand. Moving a mark keeps its remaining uses and expiry. A face or card may have both a helpful and harmful mark. Removing one named mark leaves the others. A normal status cleanse does not erase card marks, die marks, Corpse records, Echo Records or installed Modules unless the effect explicitly names that kind of target.

Recovery and sacrifice

One health card is one card in your hand, deck or discard pile. Recovery returns a card previously lost to damage to your discard pile; it is not a draw. Cards sacrificed, paid as costs or otherwise removed without damage cannot be recovered. Recover at most two cards per round across all types. Choose cards already lost before the current attack or damage began, and reserve the recovery when you pay: a failed paid attempt still uses that part of the two-card limit. Do not pay for recovery when there is no card to choose. Recovering a card erases its Corpse token.

Last chances

Sacrifice permanently removes an additional card from your hand or discard pile as a cost. It cannot be the card being played or a card the incoming damage would already remove, and it must leave you at least one health card. Death Anchor can save you once per battle by returning an earlier damage loss before defeat is decided; it also needs a remaining recovery use that round. Beacon instead selects your next Income draw. Cards cannot be played after defeat.

Transfers and redirects

Moving a status removes it from one creature and puts it on the other. If the destination cannot receive it, leave it where it was. Moving never duplicates a stack or refreshes its expiry. Redirecting damage moves the stated amount to the enemy instead of you; it keeps its damage type, allows prevention, and does not grant another defense roll. The same point of damage cannot be redirected twice.

Battle Hooks Field Guide · Turn structure

Shared play rules: timing, dice, costs and recovery

These rules explain the proposed mixed types. Curse and Venom keep their reviewed rules. The new statuses and mixed-dice behavior still need approval and implementation.

Reading these rules

You means the player using the type or the effect that placed a mark. This enemy means the creature carrying the harmful status. Gain puts stacks on you; apply puts them on the chosen enemy. A cap is the most stacks allowed. Keep unused stacks unless the rule says when to remove them.

When cards can be used

Action: during your Offensive planning, before choosing your ability. Offensive reaction: after the combat dice and chosen abilities are revealed, before attacks. Defensive reaction: after a Defensive ability is rolled. Roll reaction: after dice are rolled for a status, defense or card, before using their results. Application reaction: before a new status or mark is added. Damage reaction: after incoming damage and the health cards at risk are shown, before those cards are removed.

Playing and paying

Playing a card means using it from your hand, paying its Energy and any other listed costs, and putting it in discard. This counts as a play even if the opponent later cancels its effect. A card you cannot afford is not played and costs nothing. Echo repeating damage, a status activating, drawing a card and discarding a card are not plays from your hand. Pay optional ability costs when choosing the ability; resources it will give you afterward cannot pay those costs.

One creature when types mix

A mixed creature still uses five combat dice and chooses one Offensive ability. Its chosen types each provide their listed ways to earn resources, even when it has zero stacks. Gaining a status from another type gives that status its printed uses, but does not grant that other type’s separate rewards: gaining Armor does not also give the Stone way to earn Resolve. Card pools are options for building a creature, not a starting deck containing every card.

Numbers and symbols

These types use faces 1–3 for their first symbol, 4–5 for their second, and 6 for their third. Symbols belong to their type: Blood Fang is not Venom Fang. A matching-number or straight recipe can use dice from any type. Three equal numbers means at least three matching dice. A four-number straight is 1–4, 2–5 or 3–6. A die supplies only one required symbol in a recipe, even if Graft gives it another option. Rune stores a number; it does not add a symbol.

Rolling versus changing a number

Rolling a die can activate a marked face such as Curse or Boon, even if that number is later rerolled. Changing the number without rolling does not activate either mark. Use the final number for the normal status, defense or card result, including Conductive. A die rolled for one effect may be rerolled at most once and have its number changed at most once. Catalyst acts automatically before optional Soaked retries. Neither can reroll a die the other already retried.

Which die carries a mark

Combat rolls use your five combat dice. A card such as Pulse that tells an enemy to roll a combat die uses that die and its marks; the extra roll does not change their saved combat results. A Defensive ability instead uses fresh dice of its named type. Proposed mixed Curse/Venom creatures will also roll their marked combat dice for toxins: assign checks to dice 1 through 5 in order, then start again at die 1 if more checks are needed. That mixed-dice rule is a design proposal; playable Venom currently uses separate effect dice.

Extra rolls and locked dice

Normal combat rolls and their rerolls use the game’s roll limit. Overload adds one attempt, up to four total including the first roll. Entangle uses its existing reduction. Chill instead charges 1 Energy to begin rerolling. Scent explicitly allows one extra single-die attempt after the normal attempts. It can earn Charge and affect Streak, but cannot reroll a Frostbound die.

Attacks, follow-ups and prevention

An Offensive ability is your attack. Damaging Action cards join the attacks due to resolve and remain separate hits; they do not give extra ability selections. Use your one Defensive ability against one attack or damaging card. Echo, Pulse, Brood and Pounce happen as their text directs, allow damage prevention, and do not grant another Defensive roll. For one incoming hit, apply rolled defense and protective statuses first, then any halving effect, then damage-reaction card prevention.

Applying a status with an attack

Blocking attack damage does not also stop statuses or resources the ability grants. A reaction that cancels the whole ability stops those effects too. Unless the text specifically puts a mark before damage, a newly applied Blood Mark, Web or similar mark watches later damage, not the attack that just placed it. Effects saying after the attack happen after its damage and attached statuses finish.

Rewards and limits

Earn prevention rewards only for damage you actually stop: preventing 3 against a 1-damage hit counts as 1. Damage rewards require damage that gets through. Each type can earn its own reward from the same event, but its per-round limit still applies. A new reward becomes available after the action that earned it. It cannot pay for that same action. An extra damage effect cannot repeatedly activate itself through other extra damage effects.

Stack limits, expiry and cleansing

Extra stacks above the cap are lost unless a rule says otherwise; Poison retains its reviewed Incubation overflow rule. Adding a stack does not refresh older stacks. Next Offensive or next Ongoing Effects means the next one that starts after application. Following round means the round after the current one. Remove unused timed stacks when their stated expiry arrives. Cleanse removes an existing stack. Stopping a new stack prevents its application; it is not a cleanse. Once a status has produced a visible roll or damage to answer, removing the status does not undo that pending effect.

Card and die marks

Enemy card marks may target the discard pile or cards already revealed by damage, never a hidden hand or deck. Drawing a marked card does not reveal the rest of the hand. Moving a mark keeps its remaining uses and expiry. A face or card may have both a helpful and harmful mark. Removing one named mark leaves the others. A normal status cleanse does not erase card marks, die marks, Corpse records, Echo Records or installed Modules unless the effect explicitly names that kind of target.

Recovery and sacrifice

One health card is one card in your hand, deck or discard pile. Recovery returns a card previously lost to damage to your discard pile; it is not a draw. Cards sacrificed, paid as costs or otherwise removed without damage cannot be recovered. Recover at most two cards per round across all types. Choose cards already lost before the current attack or damage began, and reserve the recovery when you pay: a failed paid attempt still uses that part of the two-card limit. Do not pay for recovery when there is no card to choose. Recovering a card erases its Corpse token.

Last chances

Sacrifice permanently removes an additional card from your hand or discard pile as a cost. It cannot be the card being played or a card the incoming damage would already remove, and it must leave you at least one health card. Death Anchor can save you once per battle by returning an earlier damage loss before defeat is decided; it also needs a remaining recovery use that round. Beacon instead selects your next Income draw. Cards cannot be played after defeat.

Transfers and redirects

Moving a status removes it from one creature and puts it on the other. If the destination cannot receive it, leave it where it was. Moving never duplicates a stack or refreshes its expiry. Redirecting damage moves the stated amount to the enemy instead of you; it keeps its damage type, allows prevention, and does not grant another defense roll. The same point of damage cannot be redirected twice.

Card and die marks

Recovery and sacrifice

One health card is one card in your hand, deck or discard pile. Recovery returns a card previously lost to damage to your discard pile; it is not a draw. Cards sacrificed, paid as costs or otherwise removed without damage cannot be recovered. Recover at most two cards per round across all types. Transfers and redirects

Moving a status removes it from one creature and puts it on the other. If the destination cannot receive it, leave it where it was. Moving never duplicates a stack or refreshes its expiry. Reading these rules

Playing and paying

Put a reward on a specific physical face.

Streak

Risk a good run for a larger payoff.

Whenever you choose to reroll combat dice during Offensive, gain 1 Streak if at least one of those dice lands on Boon. Otherwise lose 1 Streak. Count the whole reroll attempt once, not each die. During Offensive, spend 3 Streak for Jackpot: gain 1 Energy and draw 2 cards. Use Jackpot once per round. Keep unused Streak.

Insurance

Protect progress when you decide to risk another reroll.

When one of your voluntary Offensive rerolls misses every Boon, you may spend 1 Insurance to keep the Streak you would have lost. It does not give Favor or turn the miss into a hit. Keep unused Insurance.

How these rules work

Type-specific limits & balance

Favor gains from Boon are capped at two rewards per round, though a Boon’s two-hit lifetime still advances after that limit. Count Streak once per voluntary reroll, regardless of how many Boons appear. Insurance preserves one otherwise-lost Streak; it gives neither damage prevention nor Favor without a card saying so.

Worked interaction

You have two Streak and one Insurance. A voluntary reroll misses every Boon, so Streak would fall to one. Spend Insurance to keep two instead. You gain no Favor from that miss. A later reroll hitting Boon can bring you to three Streak for Jackpot.

Shared play rules: timing, dice, costs and recovery

These rules explain the proposed mixed types. Curse and Venom keep their reviewed rules. The new statuses and mixed-dice behavior still need approval and implementation.

Reading these rules

You means the player using the type or the effect that placed a mark. This enemy means the creature carrying the harmful status. Gain puts stacks on you; apply puts them on the chosen enemy. A cap is the most stacks allowed. Keep unused stacks unless the rule says when to remove them.

When cards can be used

Action: during your Offensive planning, before choosing your ability. Offensive reaction: after the combat dice and chosen abilities are revealed, before attacks. Defensive reaction: after a Defensive ability is rolled. Roll reaction: after dice are rolled for a status, defense or card, before using their results. Application reaction: before a new status or mark is added. Damage reaction: after incoming damage and the health cards at risk are shown, before those cards are removed.

Playing and paying

Playing a card means using it from your hand, paying its Energy and any other listed costs, and putting it in discard. This counts as a play even if the opponent later cancels its effect. A card you cannot afford is not played and costs nothing. Echo repeating damage, a status activating, drawing a card and discarding a card are not plays from your hand. Pay optional ability costs when choosing the ability; resources it will give you afterward cannot pay those costs.

One creature when types mix

A mixed creature still uses five combat dice and chooses one Offensive ability. Its chosen types each provide their listed ways to earn resources, even when it has zero stacks. Gaining a status from another type gives that status its printed uses, but does not grant that other type’s separate rewards: gaining Armor does not also give the Stone way to earn Resolve. Card pools are options for building a creature, not a starting deck containing every card.

Numbers and symbols

These types use faces 1–3 for their first symbol, 4–5 for their second, and 6 for their third. Symbols belong to their type: Blood Fang is not Venom Fang. A matching-number or straight recipe can use dice from any type. Three equal numbers means at least three matching dice. A four-number straight is 1–4, 2–5 or 3–6. A die supplies only one required symbol in a recipe, even if Graft gives it another option. Rune stores a number; it does not add a symbol.

Rolling versus changing a number

Rolling a die can activate a marked face such as Curse or Boon, even if that number is later rerolled. Changing the number without rolling does not activate either mark. Use the final number for the normal status, defense or card result, including Conductive. A die rolled for one effect may be rerolled at most once and have its number changed at most once. Catalyst acts automatically before optional Soaked retries. Neither can reroll a die the other already retried.

Which die carries a mark

Combat rolls use your five combat dice. A card such as Pulse that tells an enemy to roll a combat die uses that die and its marks; the extra roll does not change their saved combat results. A Defensive ability instead uses fresh dice of its named type. Proposed mixed Curse/Venom creatures will also roll their marked combat dice for toxins: assign checks to dice 1 through 5 in order, then start again at die 1 if more checks are needed. That mixed-dice rule is a design proposal; playable Venom currently uses separate effect dice.

Extra rolls and locked dice

Normal combat rolls and their rerolls use the game’s roll limit. Overload adds one attempt, up to four total including the first roll. Entangle uses its existing reduction. Chill instead charges 1 Energy to begin rerolling. Scent explicitly allows one extra single-die attempt after the normal attempts. It can earn Charge and affect Streak, but cannot reroll a Frostbound die.

Attacks, follow-ups and prevention

An Offensive ability is your attack. Damaging Action cards join the attacks due to resolve and remain separate hits; they do not give extra ability selections. Use your one Defensive ability against one attack or damaging card. Echo, Pulse, Brood and Pounce happen as their text directs, allow damage prevention, and do not grant another Defensive roll. For one incoming hit, apply rolled defense and protective statuses first, then any halving effect, then damage-reaction card prevention.

Applying a status with an attack

Blocking attack damage does not also stop statuses or resources the ability grants. A reaction that cancels the whole ability stops those effects too. Unless the text specifically puts a mark before damage, a newly applied Blood Mark, Web or similar mark watches later damage, not the attack that just placed it. Effects saying after the attack happen after its damage and attached statuses finish.

Rewards and limits

Earn prevention rewards only for damage you actually stop: preventing 3 against a 1-damage hit counts as 1. Damage rewards require damage that gets through. Each type can earn its own reward from the same event, but its per-round limit still applies. A new reward becomes available after the action that earned it. It cannot pay for that same action. An extra damage effect cannot repeatedly activate itself through other extra damage effects.

Stack limits, expiry and cleansing

Extra stacks above the cap are lost unless a rule says otherwise; Poison retains its reviewed Incubation overflow rule. Adding a stack does not refresh older stacks. Next Offensive or next Ongoing Effects means the next one that starts after application. Following round means the round after the current one. Remove unused timed stacks when their stated expiry arrives. Cleanse removes an existing stack. Stopping a new stack prevents its application; it is not a cleanse. Once a status has produced a visible roll or damage to answer, removing the status does not undo that pending effect.

Card and die marks

Enemy card marks may target the discard pile or cards already revealed by damage, never a hidden hand or deck. Drawing a marked card does not reveal the rest of the hand. Moving a mark keeps its remaining uses and expiry. A face or card may have both a helpful and harmful mark. Removing one named mark leaves the others. A normal status cleanse does not erase card marks, die marks, Corpse records, Echo Records or installed Modules unless the effect explicitly names that kind of target.

Recovery and sacrifice

One health card is one card in your hand, deck or discard pile. Recovery returns a card previously lost to damage to your discard pile; it is not a draw. Cards sacrificed, paid as costs or otherwise removed without damage cannot be recovered. Recover at most two cards per round across all types. Choose cards already lost before the current attack or damage began, and reserve the recovery when you pay: a failed paid attempt still uses that part of the two-card limit. Do not pay for recovery when there is no card to choose. Recovering a card erases its Corpse token.

Last chances

Sacrifice permanently removes an additional card from your hand or discard pile as a cost. It cannot be the card being played or a card the incoming damage would already remove, and it must leave you at least one health card. Death Anchor can save you once per battle by returning an earlier damage loss before defeat is decided; it also needs a remaining recovery use that round. Beacon instead selects your next Income draw. Cards cannot be played after defeat.

Transfers and redirects

Moving a status removes it from one creature and puts it on the other. If the destination cannot receive it, leave it where it was. Moving never duplicates a stack or refreshes its expiry. Redirecting damage moves the stated amount to the enemy instead of you; it keeps its damage type, allows prevention, and does not grant another defense roll. The same point of damage cannot be redirected twice.

Identity doctrine

Corruption that travels with the die

Curse is persistent battle state attached to a physical die face. It does not disappear after activation and does not have default charges, weak or strong variants, or an automatic duration.

The core loop

Apply Poison → overflow its cap to apply Incubation → use Catalyst to keep it alive → mature a surviving stack or cultivate it directly with Distill → exploit it with Fever Spike or Terminal Bite, or spend it when survival matters → reapply through Needlefang.

Pressure build

Keep the disease alive

Needlefang + Incubation + Catalyst. Keep applying Poison to reach overflow, then preserve Poison through its next normal check so one stack can mature into Volatile Poison. Distill offers a more expensive immediate conversion.

Conversion build

Trade tomorrow for today

Extract + Coagulate + Shock Dose. Consume stacks for preparation, survival, or burst, then reapply Poison with Needlefang or delivery cards. Spending your last normal Poison before Incubation matures gives up its upgrade opportunity.

S-03 · Automatic status

Catalyst

Positive · its holder · cap 3. Persistent, starts at zero, rejects excess. Gained only by listed effects. It has no automatic Income.

Automatically spend 1 Catalyst to reroll one enemy toxin die that would remove a stack. Check all revealed enemy toxin dice in the current batch together and follow this priority:

  1. Volatile Poison showing 6: reroll one eligible die.
  2. Otherwise, normal Poison showing 5 or 6: reroll one eligible die.
  3. Neither exists: keep your Catalyst. Never reroll a Volatile Poison 5 with Catalyst; that result is not clearing a stack.

No choice or Energy payment is required. This works on natural and Provoked checks, including Terminal Bite’s Provoke 2. The enemy need not have Volatile Poison for Catalyst to protect normal Poison. Unrolled Volatile stacks do not affect priority; only dice in the current batch matter. It never targets toxin dice on its own holder.

Limits: one automatic Catalyst spend per holder per roll batch; each die can be rerolled at most once per batch across Venom effects. If the reroll still clears, the stack is removed normally. Do not spend again to retry that die. Unused Catalyst persists for later batches.

Automatic timing, eligibility, and other costs

After toxin dice roll, scan them for an enemy clearing result that has not already been rerolled. During Effects this happens automatically, before recovery and damage. Outside Effects, ordinary roll responses finish first. Already-rolled toxin dice remain eligible after cleansing because their outcomes still resolve. Do not spend Catalyst on a damaging or idle face. Consider all eligible Volatile 6 results before any eligible normal Poison 5–6 results.

Within the same priority, use the earliest die in the batch’s fixed displayed order. Process multiple eligible Catalyst holders in the engine’s fixed actor order, rechecking eligibility after each response. Spend the Catalyst immediately and physically reroll that die. Reveal the forced reroll result. It cannot be skipped or undone by passing. Effects continues automatically; no response or acknowledgement is required. This does not reset the Catalyst or per-die limits. Only the final face produces damage or recovery. There is no prompt to save an eligible automatic use for later.

Explicit Catalyst costs on other cards or abilities remain voluntary payments for their printed effects. They do not grant an additional automatic reroll. Catalyst gained before a new roll batch is available to that batch; Catalyst gained after a holder’s automatic check waits for a later batch.

S-04 · Reworked proposal

Incubation

Negative · enemy combatant · cap 1. Normally requires an enemy with at least 1 normal Poison. Shedskin’s Coil result can apply Incubation before Poison is present; a normal Poison stack must still survive until maturation for it to upgrade. Volatile Poison may already be at its cap; check room for the upgrade when Incubation matures. Venom abilities and cards can apply it by overflowing the normal Poison cap. Effects that explicitly apply Incubation, such as Slow Release and Barbed Mantle’s Coil result, do not require a full Poison stack. Reapplying while Incubation is present has no effect and does not postpone it.

After next round’s normal toxin rolls and their damage finish, upgrade 1 remaining Poison into 1 Volatile Poison. Then remove Incubation. If no normal Poison remains, or Volatile Poison is full, remove Incubation without upgrading anything.

This replaces a stack; it does not add an extra stack or exceed either status cap. The upgraded stack first rolls as Volatile Poison next round. Catalyst can help prevent the last Poison from recovering before the upgrade. Cleansing Incubation before it matures stops the upgrade.

Timing and responses

Resolve maturation at Ongoing Effects exit, after normal rolls, recovery, and damage, but before the final battle-completion check. Only Incubation already present when this Ongoing Effects segment began is eligible; new or replacement Incubation waits for the next one. It must still be present to mature. This status does not queue Poison applications at entry.

Automatically resolve the conversion without opening a player decision. Recheck that the original Incubation, a normal Poison stack, and Volatile capacity still exist. The Poison removal and Volatile application succeed together or neither happens. Remove the original Incubation after the attempt even if the conversion is blocked or has no legal target; never remove newly applied replacement Incubation. Cleansing Poison or Incubation before Effects can stop maturation. No cleansing card can interrupt Effects itself. Provoke and Terminal Bite do not advance this timer.

Removal vocabulary. “Toxin stack” means Poison or Volatile Poison only. Incubation is an infection marker, but cannot be Provoked or spent as a toxin. Catalyst is a positive resource and cannot be removed by a negative-only cleanse. Removing a status removes all its current stacks; removing one stack removes exactly one.

1
Existing status semantics

Capture before editing

All scheduled effects at the same checkpoint read the same pre-batch state. Removing Poison during its roll window changes future stacks; it does not erase already collected rolls. Applying or converting a stack during that window does not change the status definition captured for a die.

2
Extra normal Poison checks

Provoke N

Choose up to N existing Poison or Volatile Poison stacks on one enemy. That enemy rolls one D6 for each chosen stack now, using its normal damage and recovery rules. Provoke 1 checks one stack; Provoke 2 checks up to two different stacks. You may choose either status or a mixture. If the enemy has no toxin stacks, there is nothing to check.

For normal Poison, 1–4 deals 1 damage and keeps the stack; 5–6 removes it. For Volatile Poison, 1–4 deals 2 damage, 5 does nothing, and 6 removes it. Each batch allows roll reactions, then damage responses. Provoke brings a normal check forward; it does not guarantee extra lifetime damage or activate Incubation.

3
Shared Provoke limit

Two Provoked checks per target per round

All Provoke sources on both sides share a limit of two Provoked checks against the same afflicted actor each round. Mark those uses when the action is accepted; canceled checks do not refund them. Fever Spike and Terminal Bite mark their uses after their final ability and tier are settled. Reset at the start of the next round. A source checks fewer stacks if fewer stacks or uses remain; Provoke-only cards require at least one legal check.

Terminal Bite uses this same allowance and the normal toxin outcome tables. Natural scheduled checks and Agitate do not use it. Agitate checks every stack of one chosen toxin even when no Provoke uses remain. Catalyst rerolls change a selected check’s result; they neither use an additional Provoke slot nor grant another outcome. Catalyst may still trigger once in each new roll batch, even within the same round.

4
Paid at acceptance

Spend existing stacks

“Spend” and a listed “remove … as a cost” immediately remove exactly the selected current stacks when play is accepted. Choose the status and amount then; the same stack cannot pay twice. Only successfully paid stacks count toward a reward. These costs are not cleanses and do not fire recovery outcomes. If the action is later canceled, spent Energy, Catalyst, stacks, and the played card stay spent.

5
Atomic exchange · new proposal

Cultivate Poison into Volatile Poison

“Convert 1 Poison” exchanges one existing Poison for one Volatile Poison on that same enemy. Require the source stack and destination capacity both when played and when committed. Link removal and application: if either is canceled, invalidated, or loses a simultaneous conflict, neither happens. Pay Energy and Catalyst at acceptance; an unsuccessful conversion does not refund those costs. Conversion requires an open Volatile slot; it cannot overflow. No exchange can use the same Poison twice. Poison overflow applies Incubation for a later upgrade; it does not immediately convert a stack.

6
Separate damage and delivery

Guard the hit, answer the infection

Unqualified “damage” on an attack or Action card is ordinary defendable damage that enters normal Defense and Damage Resolution. Natural and Provoked toxin checks, including Terminal Bite’s Provoke, create status damage. Natural Effects damage resolves automatically. Provoked damage outside Effects permits eligible damage responses. Neither creates another Defensive ability selection. Damage removes health cards by the game’s normal rules. Prevention affects only its named source; canceling an entire source also stops its attached status applications and queued effects.

Effects now runs as one automatic visual sequence. Prepare cleansing, Catalyst, and other protection before Effects. Bitter Reagent now grants 1 Catalyst for 1 Energy; Measured Dose grants 2 for 2 Energy. Neither edits a revealed die. General dice manipulation cannot target Effects dice. Poison overflow → Incubation.

Whenever a Venom ability or card applies normal Poison to an enemy, fill available Poison slots first, up to cap 3. If at least one applied stack cannot fit because of that cap, automatically apply 1 Incubation if the enemy has no Incubation. No extra Energy or Catalyst is paid.

One overflow or several gives the same single marker. If Incubation is already present, the excess is lost. A full Volatile Poison cap does not prevent applying Incubation. Never add stacks to Incubation, refresh its timer, or upgrade Poison immediately. Merely reaching 3 Poison without any excess does not trigger overflow. Volatile Poison applications do not use this rule.

Application timing and responses

Determine overflow when the normal Poison application resolves, after its responses and normal simultaneous-conflict handling. Count only stacks rejected because of the Poison cap. Canceled, prevented, or otherwise invalid applications do not generate overflow. An uncanceled application can overflow even if all of its stacks are rejected by the cap. Use the state at resolution, not the state when the ability or card was selected.

After the Poison application resolves, eligible overflow immediately applies 1 Incubation with no separate response window. Recheck that the enemy still has normal Poison and has no Incubation before applying it. Do not check Volatile Poison capacity at application. Competing proposals can establish only one marker; the others have no effect. If Incubation is already present when overflow occurs, do not queue a replacement in case it is later cleansed. A blocked overflow marker is lost; additional excess from the same application does not retry it.

Incubation then uses its normal maturation timing. It must be present at the start of an Ongoing Effects segment to mature after that segment’s normal toxin checks. Overflow created during that segment waits for the next one. Cleansing the marker or filling the Volatile cap does not undo Poison that already fit.

Timing and ownership
  1. Action means Offensive / main / your planning window, before locking an ability. Played cards leave hand for discard and pay Energy immediately. An Action can create normal sources; it never grants an extra Offensive ability selection.
  2. Offensive reaction means the shared revealed Offensive window before source finalization. Changing a combat face changes its symbol and revalidates the selected ability and tier.
  3. Effects is automatic. Status dice roll, Catalyst retries eligible results, damage removes cards, recovered stacks clear, Bleed decreases, and Incubation matures without player input. No cards, manual rolls, dice edits, prevention responses, or acknowledgements are legal in this segment. Agitate and Provoke outside Effects keep their normal response timing; general dice cards may still affect eligible rolls there.
  4. Resolve toxin outcomes before damage. Saves remove their stacks; damaging results then remove cards. During Effects this sequence is automatic. Outside Effects, eligible damage responses can use only the stacks still present.
  5. Defensive reaction means the shared Defensive / main response window after selected defense dice are revealed and before defense finalization. It remains available against an Offensive source even if you passed on choosing a defense. Accepted Venom defensive prevention and counterapplications finalize before Damage Resolution; Barbed Mantle’s new infection is not yet present for an earlier cleanse in that roll window to remove.
  6. Damage reaction includes an eligible status-damage or ordinary damage-card reveal. Defense-only entries explicitly say so. Cards do not create nested response windows unless their text calls for a roll or child consequence window.
  7. The afflicted actor owns its statuses. Any Venom player may pay an explicit enemy-stack cost against its chosen legal enemy; no effect silently tracks who originally applied a stack. A card’s rewards go to the player who paid for that card.
  8. Catalyst rerolls follow the one-per-die limit. At most one Venom card that sets a given toxin die’s face may be accepted per batch. A later legal reroll can still replace a set face. Normal simultaneous conflict handling decides competing edits; neither player gets unlimited last-word corrections.
Automatic priority

Volatile 6 and Poison 5

With 1 Catalyst available, automatically spend it on the Volatile 6, even if the normal Poison die appeared first. If the reroll becomes 5, Volatile stays and deals no damage. The normal Poison 5 still clears its stack; this batch’s Catalyst use is finished.

If the original results were instead Volatile 5 and Poison 6, ignore the Volatile die and automatically reroll the normal Poison. If every result is damaging or idle, no Catalyst is spent.

Worked conversion

One Poison, one Catalyst

Distill costs 1 Energy and 1 Catalyst. If the exchange commits, Poison goes from one to zero and Volatile Poison from zero to one. There is no immediate damage or free extra roll.

Conversion resolves immediately after payment. If the required Poison or Volatile capacity is absent at resolution, neither conversion operation happens; paid resources remain spent.

Play patterns and failure cases

Synergy that leaves decisions on both sides

Six internal combinations and six future pairing directions make the interactions reviewable. The numbers below are prototype values, not playtest results.

01 · Preserve → mature

Incubation + Catalyst

Start the next round with 1 Poison and 1 Incubation on the enemy, and 1 Catalyst on yourself. Their Poison rolls 5, which would clear it. With no eligible Volatile 6 in the batch, Catalyst automatically spends itself to reroll that Poison. If it becomes 2, it deals 1 damage and remains; Incubation then upgrades it to Volatile Poison at Ongoing exit. If the reroll is 5 or 6, it clears and Incubation has nothing to upgrade.

02 · Cultivate → pressure

Volatile + Fever Spike + Catalyst

Prepare Catalyst during planning with Measured Dose. Fever Spike provokes extra toxin checks after its attack. A Volatile Poison 6 automatically spends one Catalyst and rerolls; a 5 is left alone. Any remaining Catalyst can protect a later group of rolls during the next automatic Effects segment.

03 · Gain → Provoke

Terminal Bite + Catalyst priority

With 1 Poison and 1 Volatile Poison on the enemy and two Provoke uses available, Terminal Bite first grants 1 Catalyst, then deals its 4-damage attack and checks both stacks through Provoke 2. Poison rolls 5 and Volatile rolls 6. Catalyst automatically rerolls the Volatile die first. If it becomes 3, Volatile deals 2 status damage and stays; the normal Poison 5 clears. If it remains 6, both stacks clear. There is only one Catalyst use in this batch.

04 · Infection → survival

Needlefang + Coagulate

Facing 5 incoming damage with at least 1 enemy Poison, pay 1 Energy and that stack for Coagulate. The selected source drops to 2. You gave up one future Poison roll chain to save up to three health cards now.

05 · Defense → later setup

Shedskin + Extract

A Fang/Gland defense result prevents 1 and gains 1 Catalyst. In a later planning window, Extract spends 1 enemy Poison to reach 3 Catalyst. You can now budget one for Distill and retain two for later windows; the cap prevents hoarding.

06 · Card choice → ability choice

Steady Hand + Forked Tongue

A final pool of Fang/Fang/Gland/Coil/Gland qualifies the higher Fever Spike tier. Steady Hand can build the missing Gland during planning; Forked Tongue can turn a 5 into a Coil in a revealed pool. Revalidation decides the final ability, so an enemy edit can still disrupt the plan.

Future mixed-type directions · not foundational cards

Six ways the engine can connect

07 · Curse · shared roll risk

A future Fusion effect could replace one toxin effect die with a chosen cursed combat die. Face 1 would resolve that toxin and add Curse Count from the physical overlay. Requires persistent die selection and H-04; the base kit does not do this.

08 · Fortune · recovery has value

A future shared-die rule could let a Boon reward the same physical roll used for Poison. Venom wants a low damaging result; the afflicted Fortune player may prefer a beneficial face. Boon charges and toxin recovery each resolve once.

09 · Tide · relocate pressure

An explicitly granted transfer could move one Poison stack between legal combatants with capacity. Venom’s existing-target payoffs would then follow the new location. Moving a stack does not copy it or move already collected rolls.

10 · Frost · buy maturation time

A Frost effect that reduces Offensive attempts can help Venom survive Incubation’s delay. The target still receives its natural toxin checks; a frozen combat die does not freeze a separate status die without an explicit rule.

11 · Swarm · distribute doses

A future Web card could split an application of two Poison into one on each of two linked enemies. It needs multi-enemy targeting and the link hook. Total applications remain two, and each target enforces its own cap and Provoke budget.

12 · Arcane · copy the chosen result

A future Echo could copy one finalized toxin damage proposal. It must explicitly exclude recovery, new physical rolls, Catalyst gain, and another Echo. That copied damage gets its own source attribution and normal response handling.

Counterplay

Recovery, timing, and resource pressure

Cleanse the correct status

Poison, Volatile Poison, and Incubation are three different negative statuses. A “remove one status” effect removes all stacks of the selected definition, not all three definitions. Catalyst is positive.

Antidote is legal during Offensive planning and Defensive reactions. Cleanse during planning or Defensive reactions to remove stacks before the next automatic Effects segment.

Answer the actual damage source

Prevent or cancel the appropriate toxin damage proposal after results finalize. Guarding Needlefang’s direct hit does not also stop its Poison. A whole-source cancel does stop the attack’s applications and queued effects.

Terminal Bite’s direct hit and later Provoke damage are separate sources. Preventing the 4-damage attack does not cancel its Catalyst gain or checks; those checks allow normal recovery and status-damage responses. Shock Dose still consumes Volatile Poison, so preventing that card’s damage punishes a committed stack cost.

Attack preparation windows

Disrupt Gland/Coil requirements, pressure Energy, or force Catalyst to be spent defensively. Venom cannot keep purchasing certain outcomes, cultivation, and protection from a resource capped at three.

Incubation needs at least one normal Poison to survive the next round’s checks. Clear all normal Poison or cleanse Incubation before its exchange commits to stop the upgrade. A full Volatile cap also prevents conversion. Incubation then expires; it never waits indefinitely for a future target.

Let risky checks help you

Every normal Poison check has a 2-in-6 natural recovery chance; Volatile has a 1-in-6 chance. Provoke can cure the stack it was meant to exploit. Catalyst automatically retries at most one eligible result per holder per batch, prioritizing Volatile 6; other clearing results, or a failed retry, still remove stacks. After two Provoked checks against you, further requests, including Terminal Bite’s, wait until next round.

Ordinary health restoration, cleanse, and reroll tools require content that actually grants them. This dossier adds no free universal antidote or resistance action.

Lifetime damage is expected damage per normal check divided by recovery probability. Needlefang’s revised 4+1 Poison, 3+2 Poison, and 2+3 Poison therefore give expected totals of 6, 7, and 8 when all new stacks fit. These 6 / 7 / 8 totals describe fresh stacks that all fit; they do not include Incubation. Applying into a full Poison cap can now add Incubation if eligible, giving continued Poison delivery a delayed upgrade payoff. Additional overflow has no value while Incubation is already present. A full Volatile Poison cap still allows the marker; a slot must open by maturation for the upgrade to succeed.

Provoke brings a normal check forward and includes its recovery risk. Without other modifiers, it changes when damage may happen rather than increasing a stack’s expected lifetime damage. Terminal Bite first gains 1 Catalyst, then deals 4 direct damage and Provokes up to two stacks within the shared allowance. Those checks create normal status damage and can remove stacks. Its Catalyst retries at most one eligible clearing result automatically, prioritizing Volatile Poison. For one isolated checked stack with Catalyst available and no other effects, recovery on that check requires two clearing results: 1/9 for normal Poison or 1/36 for Volatile Poison. These reduced chances do not apply to every die in a mixed batch; only one eligible die receives the automatic retry.

Incubation upgrades only one surviving normal Poison and expires after one attempt. With no reactions or other removals, its chance of having at least one survivor is 2/3 with one Poison, 8/9 with two, and 26/27 with three. More Poison therefore improves reliability without a hard three-stack requirement for direct Incubation effects. Overflow starts at the full Poison cap, but the enemy can still cleanse Poison or Incubation before maturation. Volatile’s expected lifetime damage of 8 versus normal Poison’s 2 makes this a substantial reward; the delay, recovery risk, single-marker cap, and conversion counterplay are the proposed checks on its strength.

Review priorities: Needlefang’s immediate-versus-delayed tier choice; conversion cost relative to +1 Energy Income; how often the three-Coil finisher is selected; Catalyst scarcity after defense; automatic Catalyst priority in mixed batches and the response time from up to six natural checks plus two Provoked checks in a round; and whether repeatable overflow produces Volatile Poison too cheaply or too reliably at Incubation cap one, and whether Slow Release and Incubate remain worthwhile as earlier maturation tools.

No simulation, match win-rate estimate, or game implementation is claimed. Start playtesting with the stated caps and costs; change one pressure point at a time.
